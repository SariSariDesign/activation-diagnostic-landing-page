Findings

Top 5 Findings

The 5 highest-impact friction points, in the order a user hits them, grouped by page across the flow. Selected from a 17-finding evaluation across 10 UX dimensions. Select "See more details" on any finding for the evidence and fix.

Homepage

1
Interaction Design Critical

The hero search gives no response when users press Enter or the search button

In our automated test sessions, typing a skin name into the hero search and pressing Enter did nothing: no results page, no error, no movement. The orange search button did the same, and the suggestion dropdown showed only an echo of the typed query, with no skin suggestions and no loading indicator. The search results page itself works when opened directly by its address, so the backend is healthy. The search box is failing to hand the query off.

Why This Finding Matters

This is the single promoted action on the page and the top of your funnel. A visitor who types "karambit" and gets silence does not stop to diagnose your JavaScript. They leave, and the affiliate click-out that pays for everything never happens. We reproduced this on two separate page loads; it deserves a manual check on real hardware today.
IXD-01 N-01 ONB-03
Recommendation
  • Make Enter and the arrow button always navigate to the search results page for the typed query, as a plain form submission fallback that works even if other scripts fail.
  • Show a "Searching..." row inside the suggestion dropdown while suggestions load, and a "No skins found" row when nothing matches.
  • Move the suggestion lookup off the main thread (or debounce it) so typing never freezes the page.
Effort: Low Impact: High
2
Mobile UX Major

The mobile layout cuts off the headline, nav, and footer at common phone widths

At a 360 px viewport, a standard Android width, the hero headline renders as "Compare C..." with its right side cut off, the Login button is half clipped, the logo shows as a sliver, and the footer columns run off both edges. The item page has the same overflow: the item title is clipped and the breadcrumb wraps awkwardly.

Why This Finding Matters

Your audience lives on their phones and judges fast. A first-time mobile visitor cannot read the value proposition at all, and clipped UI reads as "unfinished" to a scam-wary crowd deciding whether to trust your prices with their money.
MOB-04 ACC-09 VD-01
Recommendation
  • Scale the hero type responsively (clamp-based font sizes) so the headline wraps instead of overflowing.
  • Audit every fixed-width container at 320 to 400 px and add the missing breakpoint styles for the nav, footer, and item header.
Effort: Medium Impact: High
3
Conversion (CRO) Major

The Trustpilot badge links to a different company's reviews

The hero's "Trustpilot 4.6 Excellent" badge, your most prominent trust signal, links to trustpilot.com/review/maestroai.io, a review page for an unrelated brand.

Why This Finding Matters

Skin traders verify. The exact users you most need to convince are the ones most likely to click that badge, and what they find looks like a fabricated rating. Your own FAQ leads with "Is Skinscanner.gg safe and legitimate?", so a broken trust cue in the hero works directly against the answer.
CRO-06 VD-08
Recommendation
  • Point the badge at SkinScanner's own Trustpilot profile, and show only the score that profile actually displays.
  • If your profile is thin, link it anyway and run a review-collection push through the Discord community.
Effort: Low Impact: High
4
Conversion (CRO) Major

Third-party gambling-style banners sit above your own content on every page

A full-width animated banner for another site ("CS2SKIN.COM 100% DEPOSIT BONUS!") sits directly under the nav on the homepage, above your hero. Item pages carry a giveaway banner in the same slot, a second banner inserted between the price listings, and two more in the right rail. A "3 FREE CASES" banner appears twice on the homepage.

Why This Finding Matters

The first pixels a new visitor sees belong to someone else's deposit-bonus offer, which competes with your search bar for the first click and pulls high-intent traffic off-site before activation. Case-opening creatives also sit awkwardly next to a "safe and legitimate" positioning. The ad revenue is real, but the current placement spends trust and click-outs to earn it.
CRO-03 VD-08 N-08
Recommendation
  • Move third-party placements below the fold and out of the listings column, capped at one per view.
  • Keep the slot under the nav for SkinScanner's own messaging: rewards, watchlist, Discord.
  • Treat the price-listings area as ad-free; it is the product.
Effort: Medium Impact: Medium

Sign-up page

5
Forms & Conversion Major

Sign-up asks for five typed fields but skips the one account every CS2 player has

Register offers Discord or email only; there is no Steam sign-in, the convention across this vertical. The email path asks for Full Name, Username, Email, Password, and Confirm Password, all labeled only by placeholder text that disappears as you type, with no password rules shown before typing and no required-field markers.

Why This Finding Matters

Every extra field costs signups on a form guarding a free feature. Steam sign-in is one click, pre-verifies a real CS2 player, and opens the door to inventory-linked features later. The current form asks a trader to type more to save a watchlist than some marketplaces ask before a purchase.
CRO-08 FORM-01 FORM-05 FORM-07
Recommendation
  • Add "Sign in through Steam" as the primary option on register and login, above Discord.
  • Drop the Full Name and Confirm Password fields; the show-password toggle already covers typos.
  • Use persistent visible labels above each field and state the password rules before the user types.
Effort: Medium Impact: High
What The Research Says

Patterns that bear on this flow

Documented research and case studies matched to the weakest areas of this flow: what moved the numbers for comparable products.

Users need a response within one second

0.1s / 1s / 10s

Nielsen's response-time limits: acknowledge input within 0.1 seconds, keep feedback flowing for anything over 1 second, and never leave a user without status past 10 seconds. The hero search broke all three in our tests, with typed queries producing no acknowledgment at all. A loading row in the dropdown and a guaranteed route to the results page bring search back inside these limits.

Source: Nielsen Norman Group, response-time limits (Sari Sari research library, interaction design)

Forms that follow the basics nearly double error-free submissions

78% vs 42%

CHI 2014 research (Seckler et al.) measured 78 percent of users submitting a form without errors on the first try when it followed usability best practices: persistent labels, minimal fields, requirements stated upfront. Forms that violated them dropped to 42 percent. SkinScanner's sign-up currently sits on the wrong side of all three of those basics.

Source: Seckler et al., CHI 2014 (Sari Sari research library, forms design)
