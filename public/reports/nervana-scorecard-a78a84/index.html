Nervana

trynervana.com  ·  Website checkout and iOS app  ·  September 17, 2026

Why This Matters

About 3 in 10 new profiles start a trial within 14 days, and the first conversation with the coach is where paying members are made: members with a meaningful first call paid at 32.6%, against 17.6% for those who skipped it and 4.8% for those whose call didn't complete (client-provided). In this walkthrough, the path to that conversation ran through a device switch, an eight-screen story, repeated symptom questions, a free AI session that never replied, and a lesson, cost survey and check-in before Coaching unlocked. The findings below are ordered by where members meet them, so the fixes can start at the top of the funnel and work down.

Website checkout

1
Conversion Rate Optimization Major

Choosing the monthly plan cuts the free trial from 7 days to 3

On the website, the plan step leads with the annual plan. Choosing the flexible, no-commitment monthly option changes the trial to three days. In the walkthrough, the shorter trial read like an error until the tester connected it to the plan choice.
Why This Finding Matters

Trial length is part of the offer a member agrees to at checkout. 59.7% of observed trial cancellations happened within 24 hours (client-provided), so a surprise in the trial terms lands right where members decide whether to stay.

CRO-10N-05
Recommendation
  • Show the trial length on each plan option before it is selected, for example "7 days free" and "3 days free".
  • Add one line under the plan choice explaining why the trial differs by plan.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Website to app handoff

2
Interaction Design Major

After paying on the website, members have to move to their phone to start

After checkout, the confirmation explains that coaching sessions and practices live in the mobile app, not on the website. The member scans a code with their phone, installs the app and signs in again with the checkout email.
Why This Finding Matters

The member has just paid and is ready to begin, and the next step is switching devices. Website visits are not yet linked to profiles (client-provided), so drop-off at this handoff is not measured today.

CRO-08IXD-05CRO-10
Recommendation
  • Offer "Text me the app link" next to the QR code on the confirmation page.
  • Send a one-tap sign-in link to the checkout email so the app opens already signed in.
  • Track app installs and first app opens after web checkout as their own funnel step.
Effort: Med Impact: High

App Store listing

3
Visual & Brand Design Minor

The App Store listing looks different from the website

The App Store screenshots use the app's dark palette, while the website uses a lighter look. In the walkthrough, the tester paused on the listing to wonder which look the app would have.
Why This Finding Matters

A consistent look across the handoff reassures a member who just paid that they are in the right place.

VD-07
Recommendation
  • Lead the App Store screenshots with the same headline and imagery the website uses, then move into the dark app screens.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Intro story

4
Onboarding & Activation Major

Eight story screens run before the app asks its first question

After Begin, the app plays an eight-screen story: "You are not alone", "The search for relief can take a toll", how Nervana works, the clinical advisory board, and a founder video with Education, Career and Personal sections. In the walkthrough, the founder's credentials read more like a professional profile than an app screen.
Why This Finding Matters

The founder video and advisory board are real trust assets. Placed up front, they push the first thing a member does for themselves, answering a question, to screen ten.

ONB-02ONB-03
Recommendation
  • Cut the story before the first question to three screens: what Nervana is, how a session works, and who built it.
  • Keep the founder video, and move the Education, Career and Personal details to a card on Today or the Coaches tab.
Effort: Med Impact: High
5
Visual & Brand Design Minor

The clinical advisory board shows three men, while most members are women

"Developed with experts" lists three physicians on the clinical advisory board, all men.
Why This Finding Matters

316 of 512 new profiles and 115 of 164 trial starters since April are women (client-provided). Seeing a clinician who reflects them is one of the quickest trust signals this screen can give.

VD-08CRO-07
Recommendation
  • Add a woman clinician who already works with Nervana to this screen, as an advisor or a featured clinician.
Effort: Low Impact: Med
6
Information Architecture Minor

The progress bar disappears when the questions start

A progress bar runs along the bottom of the story screens, with the founder screen at step eight. It disappears on "What are your main goals?" and does not return for the rest of onboarding.
Why This Finding Matters

The intake after the story is longer than the story itself. Without a progress bar, members can't tell how much is left.

IA-03N-01
Recommendation
  • Run one progress indicator from the first story screen through the free AI session.
Effort: Low Impact: Med
7
Visual & Brand Design Minor

Some story visuals don't match what they illustrate

On "Your path with Nervana", the goal "Your nervous system settles and symptoms begin to ease" uses a shield with a check mark. In the walkthrough, the tester also noted a gauge illustration whose indicator sat off-center.
Why This Finding Matters

This is the screen that introduces the coach and how the program works, so small mismatches draw attention away from the message.

VD-06VD-04
Recommendation
  • Use icons from one set whose meaning matches the line they sit beside.
  • Center the gauge indicator on the learned-pattern screen.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Goals and fit screener

8
Content & Copywriting Major

The fit screener is dense, and checking nothing leads to a stop screen before sign-up

"Which patterns feel familiar?" lists nine statements, several long and personal, such as "If you learned that a child you care about was growing up exactly as you did, you would feel sad or angry." With nothing checked, Continue opened "Let's check one thing first", which recommends seeing a healthcare provider before continuing. With any pattern checked, the result reads "You're in the right place", and in the walkthrough it wasn't clear whether any answer would lead there.
Why This Finding Matters

This screen comes before account creation, so anyone who stops here leaves with no account and no way for Nervana to follow up. A member who is unsure gets the same response as someone the screen is meant to redirect.

COPY-06COPY-07N-05FORM-01
Recommendation
  • Add "None of these" and "I'm not sure" options, and route "I'm not sure" forward with a short explanation.
  • Shorten each statement to one plain line, and keep the longer detail behind "Why we ask".
  • Let members create an account from the provider recommendation screen so they can return after seeing their provider.
  • Reflect the member's own answers on "You're in the right place", for example the patterns they checked.
Effort: Low Impact: High
9
Forms & Input Major

Symptom questions come up three times before the first session

Before sign-up, the app asks for goals and which patterns feel familiar. After sign-up, it asks for symptoms by category, the primary symptom and how long it has lasted. In the walkthrough, the goals screen also felt like a repeat of the symptom quiz on the website.
Why This Finding Matters

The symptom quiz has been a main website call to action since late August (client-provided), so many members answer similar questions twice. Each repeat adds time before the coach and suggests the app didn't keep what they already said.

FORM-01ONB-07
Recommendation
  • Pass website quiz answers into the app, and skip or pre-fill the matching questions.
  • Combine goals and patterns into a single screen.
Effort: Med Impact: Med

Sign up

10
Forms & Input Major

Creating an account also subscribes the member to the founder's newsletter

Under the password field, the sign-up screen reads "By creating an account, you'll also receive Finding Nervana, Nora's newsletter. Unsubscribe anytime." There is no way to decline.
Why This Finding Matters

A few screens later, the app promises that a member's story stays theirs. An automatic sign-up at account creation works against that promise right before the health questions begin.

FORM-01VD-08
Recommendation
  • Add an unchecked newsletter opt-in to the sign-up screen, and apply it to Google and Apple sign-in too.
  • Offer the newsletter again after a member's first week.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

Forms & Input Minor

Sign-up fields use placeholder text instead of labels

First name, Last name, Email and Password appear only as placeholder text inside each field, and disappear once the member starts typing.
Why This Finding Matters

Members who switch apps to find a password or pause mid-form lose track of which field is which.

FORM-05
Recommendation
  • Put a persistent label above each field.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Intake and setup

12
Onboarding & Activation Major

Symptoms and history are collected before the privacy promise and the Terms

After sign-up, the app asks for date of birth, gender, symptoms, the primary symptom and how long it has lasted. "Your story stays yours" and the required Terms checkbox come after all of that. On that screen, "De-identified" says Nervana follows clinical standards without naming them, and the "Nervana is not medical care" card is dimmed.
Why This Finding Matters

Nervana already has the reassurance a health buyer looks for. It arrives after the member has shared their most sensitive details, and one of its lines is vague enough to raise the question it is meant to answer.

ONB-07FORM-01COPY-08
Recommendation
  • Move "Your story stays yours" and the Terms checkbox to directly after sign-up, before date of birth.
  • Name the standard behind "De-identified", or describe what de-identification means in one plain line.
  • Show the "Nervana is not medical care" card at full contrast.
Effort: Low Impact: High
13
Content & Copywriting Minor

The 90% proof point and the refund both land at session ten

The member experience screen reads "90% of people who use Nervana find it helpful by their 10th session", with no source shown. The money-back promise asks members to complete 10 Nervana AI sessions first. In the walkthrough, the specific number read as honest, and ten sessions read as a long commitment at the point of signing up.
Why This Finding Matters

At the recommended one to three sessions a week, session ten comes weeks after a 3-day or 7-day trial has ended, so the strongest reason to stay sits past the billing decision.

COPY-08CRO-10
Recommendation
  • Add a tap-to-expand note on who was asked for the 90% figure, how many, and when.
  • Pair it with something members can reach inside the trial, such as what members notice after their first session.
Effort: Low Impact: Med
14
Forms & Input Minor

Choosing "Other" for attribution requires free text that capitalizes like a name

On "Where did you hear about Nervana?", choosing Other opens a required "Please specify" field. The keyboard capitalizes every word, as it would for a name.
Why This Finding Matters

Source attribution is mostly self-reported: 417 profiles answered the question and 53 have no known source (client-provided). Clinician referrals pay at 40.8% against 16.1% for social (client-provided), so clean answers here shape where Nora spends outreach time.

FORM-01FORM-07MOB-05
Recommendation
  • Make sure the list covers the common answers, such as clinician, friend or family, and podcast, so fewer members need Other.
  • Make "Please specify" optional, and turn off word capitalization in that field.
Effort: Low Impact: Med
15
Forms & Input Minor

"How long has this been going on?" has no option for not sure

The duration question offers only years and months. In the walkthrough, the tester wasn't sure and entered an estimate.
Why This Finding Matters

Many members with long-running symptoms don't know when they started, and a forced guess makes this answer less useful for personalization.

FORM-01FORM-06
Recommendation
  • Add a "Not sure" option alongside years and months.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Interaction Design Minor

The symptom picker shows selections two different ways

In the symptom picker, a category with one selected symptom showed only an outline around its card, while a category with two or more showed a count.
Why This Finding Matters

Members scanning the list can miss a category where they already picked something.

IXD-01N-04

Free AI session

17
Bugs & Defects Critical

The free AI session didn't respond, and onboarding carried on as if it had

The tester tapped Start Free Conversation, allowed the microphone and spoke. The orb glowed, but the coach never replied. After ending the call, the app showed "Analyzing Your Conversation" and then "Your plan is ready."
Why This Finding Matters

This is the member's first conversation with the coach. Members with a meaningful first call paid at 32.6% within 14 days, and members who started a call without a meaningful completion paid at 4.8% (client-provided). A silent call followed by a plan built on it reads as broken at the moment the product is meant to prove itself.

N-01N-09IXD-01
Recommendation
  • Show whether the coach is listening, thinking or speaking, and show a clear message if audio doesn't connect within a few seconds.
  • Offer "Try again" and "Switch to chat" when the coach doesn't reply.
  • Skip "Analyzing Your Conversation" when nothing was said, and log these calls as failed rather than completed.
Effort: Med Impact: High
18
Visual & Brand Design Minor

The coach's orb reads like a pill and isn't introduced

A glowing round orb sits at the top of most onboarding screens. It isn't named as the coach, and up close its sound-wave marks look like a pill imprint.
Why This Finding Matters

For members who have tried many treatments, an image that resembles medication sends a mixed signal in a program about retraining the nervous system.

VD-06N-02
Recommendation
  • Introduce the orb the first time it appears, for example "This is your Nervana coach."
  • Animate the marks as a waveform so they read as sound.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Plan ready and reminders

19
Interaction Design Major

The App Store rating prompt fires before the member has used the app

The iOS prompt "Enjoying Nervana? Tap a star to rate it on the App Store" appears over "Your plan is ready", before the first lesson or coaching session. In the walkthrough, the tester dismissed it because there was nothing to rate yet.
Why This Finding Matters

iOS allows this prompt only a few times a year. Spending it here gives up the chance to ask right after a good coaching session, when a rating would help App Store conversion.

ONB-07IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Ask for a rating after a member finishes their first full coaching session and debrief.
  • Show it only to members who gave positive feedback on that session.

"Your plan is ready" says "Use Nervana AI a few times each week to practice safety, reflect, and notice patterns." In the walkthrough, "practice safety" read as either practicing feeling safe or practicing safely.

This is the one line that tells members what the plan asks of them.

Tour

21
Onboarding & Activation Major

A notification request and a five-slide tour stack up before Today

A reminders screen and the iOS notification dialog come first. Today then opens under a five-slide modal: how we'll work together, preparation, the call, next steps and human coaching. Slides only move forward, so the tester couldn't return to the first one, and in the walkthrough the amount of information at once felt like a lot to remember.
Why This Finding Matters

Many members arrive anxious about their symptoms. Loading the whole program at once is harder to take in than learning each step when it arrives, and Today already shows the Prepare, Session, Debrief track.

ONB-01N-03IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Replace the tour with a one-line hint on each Today step the first time it appears.
  • If a tour stays, cut it to three slides and allow swiping back.
  • Show the reminders screen after the first lesson, when "One small practice is ready" has something behind it.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

Today

22
Onboarding & Activation Major

The first coaching session sits behind a lesson, a cost survey and a check-in

Today opens with "We'll start with some preparation before our call." The active step is a seven-part lesson, with Care Burden Baseline and Weekly Check In locked below it and no reason given. Closing the lesson early returns to Today, where the lesson is still the only way forward. Coaching appears only after all three are done, and by the check-in the tester said it felt like preparing for something that hadn't arrived yet.
Why This Finding Matters

The coach is what members are trialing. 24.3% of new profiles complete a coaching session within 7 days (client-provided), and each required step before it is a place a member can stop partway through a trial.

ONB-02ONB-03ONB-07IA-10
Recommendation
  • Unlock Coaching right after onboarding, and offer the lesson as optional preparation.
  • Move Care Burden Baseline to after the first coaching session.
  • On each locked card, say what unlocks it and how long it takes.
Effort: High Impact: High
23
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Minor

The weekly goal says "0 of 1" and marks three options as recommended

The weekly goal ring reads "0 of 1" without saying one of what. In the goal picker, 1, 2 and 3 sessions a week all carry a Recommended tag.
Why This Finding Matters

Members have to work out the unit and choose between three recommendations on their first visit to Today.

N-06IXD-04
Recommendation
  • Label the ring "0 of 1 sessions".
  • Tag one default as recommended, and keep 2 to 3 sessions as "for extra support".
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Lesson

24
Content & Copywriting Major

The lesson reads like an article, with narration that repeats the text

The Missing Link In Recovery has seven parts. Each has a Listen player that reads the paragraphs below it word for word, without saying the text is the transcript, and there is no playback speed control. Most parts pair long text with a header image; "So How Does It Actually Work?" is the one diagram.
Why This Finding Matters

The lesson is required before the first coaching session, and 27.9% of new profiles complete a lesson within 7 days (client-provided). Concepts like fight-or-flight and brain-body signals are easier to grasp as short visuals than as paragraphs.

COPY-06COPY-13VD-06
Recommendation
  • Label the text as the transcript, so members know they can listen or read.
  • Add playback speed options.
  • Turn each part's main concept into a short diagram like "So How Does It Actually Work?"
Effort: Med Impact: Med

Finished lessons leave Today with no pointer to where they went

After the check-in, the lesson card is replaced by the Coaching card. Today doesn't say where to find the lesson again.
Why This Finding Matters

Members who want to revisit an idea before their next session have to go looking for it.

IA-06ONB-04
Recommendation

Effort: Low Impact: Med

Care Burden Baseline

26
Forms & Input Major

A cost survey sits in the preparation step before the first session

Care Burden Baseline asks about urgent care or ER visits, appointments, missed days and out-of-pocket spending over 90 days. Its intro says the answers support making Nervana lower-cost or covered by insurance, and shows four icons (Time, Care, Missed life, Spending) with no explanation. In the walkthrough, the questions assumed the member already thinks of these experiences as symptoms.
Why This Finding Matters

Its stated purpose is lower cost and insurance coverage, which matters, but it sits between the member and their first coaching session. Financial questions early in a health flow cost trust.

ONB-07FORM-01COPY-07
Recommendation
  • Move Care Burden Baseline to after the first coaching session, or into week two.
  • Add "Not sure" to each question.
  • Explain the four icons, or remove them.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

First coaching session

27
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Major

The coach has no way out when a member doesn't see their symptoms as mind-body

In the first coaching session, the tester described their pain in physical, structural terms more than once. The coach returned to a nervous-system explanation each time, and the session offers no way for the member to say the framing doesn't fit.
Why This Finding Matters

Onboarding asks members to rule out structural causes first, and one member wrote after a voice session that it "makes me feel heard and understood" (client-provided). When the coach keeps returning to one explanation, the same member can feel unheard in the session meant to prove the product.

N-02N-03
Recommendation
  • Give the coach a path for members who attribute symptoms to a diagnosed physical cause: acknowledge it, point to their clinician, and offer a practice that doesn't depend on agreeing.
  • Add a "This doesn't fit me" option during or after the session.
  • Review session transcripts where a member repeats the same point three or more times.
Effort: Med Impact: High
28
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Major

The voice session has no mute, pause or timer

During the 10 to 15 minute voice session, the screen shows the orb, a background brush and End Conversation. There is no mute, no pause and no time elapsed. The walkthrough noted fast replies, and background noise didn't interrupt the call.
Why This Finding Matters

Members take these sessions at home, where interruptions happen. With no mute or pause, ending the session is the only option.

N-03N-01
Recommendation
  • Add mute and pause controls.
  • Show time elapsed against the 10 to 15 minute session.
Effort: Med Impact: Med
29
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Minor

The coach voice is chosen before the first conversation and can't be changed from the session

Members pick a voice from short previews during onboarding. In the session, the calm, slow voice felt mismatched once the conversation got harder, and the connect screen notes the session mode can't be changed once started.
Why This Finding Matters

A voice that suits a breathing exercise can feel slow in a problem-solving conversation, and members won't know which they need until they talk.

N-07IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Let members change voice and pace on the connect screen before each session.
Effort: Low Impact: Low

Session debrief

30
Bugs & Defects Major

Debrief items mark themselves done

Saving the action items as a PDF marked the action item Done, though the practice hadn't been tried. Opening Session Analysis from Today also marked it complete, and Today then showed all four debrief cards checked.
Why This Finding Matters

"Any completed action" and "completed actions on two dates" are two of Nervana's candidate activation measures (client-provided). If opening or saving counts as completing, those numbers overstate activation.

N-01N-05
Recommendation
  • Mark an action item done only when the member taps "Mark as done".
  • Mark debrief cards complete only after a clear finishing action, such as saving a reflection.
  • Check the events behind the candidate activation measures for other automatic completions.
Effort: Low Impact: High
31
Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Minor

Three separate save or print buttons, and no back arrow on Session Analysis

Session Analysis, the transcript and Action Items each have their own save or print button. Session Analysis has Continue but no back arrow.
Why This Finding Matters

Members can't tell which button saves what, or how to leave the analysis without moving forward.

N-04N-03
Recommendation
  • Replace the three buttons with one "Save session summary" that includes all three.
  • Add a back arrow to Session Analysis.
Effort: Low Impact: Low
Methodology

An AI-assisted analyst reviewed a narrated designer walkthrough of Nervana's website checkout and iOS app, from plan selection through the first coaching session and debrief, across 10 UX dimensions of a Nielsen Norman Group grounded framework, with client-provided analytics used to prioritize.

Evaluation Lens

Nervana has to show that its AI coach is worth paying for inside a short trial, to adults who have often tried a lot already. Each step was judged by whether it moves a new member toward a meaningful first conversation with the coach, and whether members come away feeling heard.

This audit is based on a designer walkthrough of Nervana's website checkout and iOS app, recorded September 8, 2026, together with client-provided analytics. The walkthrough used one test account on one iPhone. Direct access to the product, supporting flows, analytics, and user data would enable a deeper, more comprehensive review.
