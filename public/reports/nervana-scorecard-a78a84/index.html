Activation Scorecard

Nervana

trynervana.com  ·  iOS app and website checkout  ·  September 24, 2026

Why This Matters

About 3 in 10 new accounts start a trial within 14 days, and about 6 in 10 trials become paying members (client-provided). The biggest measured loss is between account creation and the trial, which is exactly the stretch this scorecard rates. Members who have a real first conversation with the coach start trials and pay at roughly twice the rate of members who skip it, so the path to that call, and what happens right after it, is where this score can move fastest. In the walkthrough it took 20 minutes to reach the first call and another 30 minutes on the Today screen before the first coaching session, inside a trial that lasts 3 days on the monthly plan.

How Nervana scores today

Friction-Heavy, but Fixable

Measured against best-practice activation patterns for health and wellness products, your flow carries friction at several points where users decide whether to continue. Products in this range usually see meaningful signup-to-activation drop-off, and it is the recoverable kind. The top findings in this scorecard are the places to start.

What the Curable pass showed. Curable opens straight into a text chat with its guide, “Clara”, and asks for email and password inside that chat about a minute in, before any value and with no password-manager support (FORM-01, FORM-05). Symptom questions are free text with a tap to continue after every message, and nothing says how long the questions will take (IXD-05, ONB-03). Then it does the thing Nervana’s flow makes members wait for: about four minutes in, it asks whether symptoms are bothering you right now and offers a short guided relief practice, which can be closed with one tap into a congratulatory “all done” (ONB-02, N-03). The paywall follows at roughly minute six, hands off to a web page, shows one annual price with no monthly breakdown, and returns the member to the app with a single button (CRO-10). The first-run tour is short, skippable, and points at the panic button and the four areas of the app rather than explaining everything (ONB-01, IA-10). No defects were observed. The evaluator’s summary: landed on the home screen knowing what to do next, though the chat is text on a screen with no voice and little personalization. The comparison that matters for Nervana is time to first value: Curable reached a relief practice in about four minutes and its offer in six, Nerva reflected the member’s answers back at three and reached its offer at six and a half; Nervana’s walkthrough reached the free AI session at minute 20.

What the Nerva pass showed. Nerva also asks for an account before it has shown anything (ONB-07), and its name fields are faded enough to be hard to read (FORM-05). From there it does three things Nervana’s onboarding doesn’t. At minute two it asks whether a healthcare professional referred you, answers “that’s okay” if not, and offers an access code at the paywall for members who came through a clinician. At minute three it reflects the member’s own answers back (“your experience fits the common DGBI pattern”) and interleaves short education tied to those answers, so the questions feel heard rather than collected (ONB-02, COPY-02). Before the offer it sets the program’s shape in plain terms (six weeks, 15 minutes a day, at least five days a week), asks for one goal, and turns it into a dated commitment the member holds to confirm, which frames the price before the price appears (CRO-10). The paywall lands at about six and a half minutes with a 7-day trial, cancel anytime, a satisfaction guarantee, and Apple Pay through Stripe. The clinical baseline (validated symptom scale, mood questions, a research questionnaire) comes after payment and before the first session, the same gating pattern as Nervana’s Care Burden Baseline, but explained as the measure progress will be checked against and asked once. The evaluator’s summary: a lot of screens, but tidbits of value along the way and a sense of being heard. Nothing on the way says how many questions remain (ONB-03), and no defects were observed. Nerva is IBS-specific, so its symptom set is narrower than Nervana’s; the flow shape is what the comparison is about.

Friction Areas

Priority Friction Areas

The six highest-priority friction points on the signup to first coaching session flow, covered in depth — in the order a user hits them, grouped by page. Each is evidence behind a dimension rating above. Select "View Details" on any card for the evidence and fix. Every other issue we logged is listed under Additional Findings below.

Intro story and intake

1
Onboarding & Activation Major

Twenty minutes of story and questions run before the first conversation with the coach

After Begin, the app plays an eight-screen story (“You are not alone”, how Nervana works, the clinical advisory board, and a founder video with Education, Career and Personal sections) before its first question. Goals and patterns follow, then sign-up, then date of birth, symptoms by category, primary symptom, duration, a proof point, privacy, attribution and voice choice. In the walkthrough the first question arrived on screen ten and the free AI session at minute 20. Several questions repeat what the website symptom quiz already asked.

Why This Finding Matters

The first call is the moment Nora’s own data points to: members with a meaningful first call paid at 33% within 14 days, against 17% for members who skipped it (client-provided). Every screen before it is a place to leave, and the aligned screen events show the welcome preview, questionnaire and symptom picker are the three slowest steps, with median view-to-completion times of 52, 54 and 82 seconds (client-provided). The founder video and advisory board are real trust assets; placed this early, they delay the thing that actually earns the trial.
ONB-02 ONB-03 ONB-07 FORM-01 IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Cut the story before the first question to three screens: what Nervana is, how a session works, and who built it. Keep the founder video; move Education, Career and Personal to a card on Today or the Coaches tab.
  • Combine goals and patterns into one screen, and keep the longer pattern statements behind “Why we ask”.
  • Ask only what the coach needs before the call: primary symptom and how long. Defer date of birth, symptom categories and attribution to after “Your plan is ready”.
  • Pass website quiz answers into the app and pre-fill or skip the matching questions.
  • Run one progress indicator from the first story screen through the free AI session.
Effort: Med Impact: High

Sign up and privacy

2
Onboarding & Activation Major

Date of birth, symptoms and history are collected before the privacy promise and the Terms, and account creation bundles a newsletter

After sign-up, the app asks for date of birth, gender, symptoms, the primary symptom and how long it has lasted. “Your story stays yours” and the required Terms checkbox come after all of that. On that screen, “De-identified” says Nervana follows clinical standards without naming them, and the “Nervana is not medical care” card is dimmed. One screen earlier, sign-up reads “By creating an account, you’ll also receive Finding Nervana, Nora’s newsletter” with no way to decline.

Why This Finding Matters

Nervana already has the reassurance a health buyer looks for. It arrives after the member has shared their most sensitive details, and one of its lines raises the question it is meant to answer. Health onboarding research puts a number on this order: an AI health companion saw 42% abandonment when sensitive data was requested before privacy was established, and recovered it by moving privacy to the front of the flow. A forced newsletter at the same moment works against the promise that follows.
ONB-07 FORM-01 FORM-05 COPY-08 VD-08
Recommendation
  • Move “Your story stays yours” and the Terms checkbox to directly after sign-up, before any health question.
  • Name the standard behind “De-identified”, or describe what de-identification means in one plain line, and show the “not medical care” card at full contrast.
  • Replace the newsletter line with an unchecked opt-in, applied to Google and Apple sign-in too. Offer it again after the first week.
  • Put a persistent label above each sign-up field.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Free AI session

3
Bugs & Defects Critical

The free AI session can be skipped in one tap, and when it didn’t connect the app carried on as if it had

“Try a free Nervana AI session” offers Start Free Conversation with a plain “Skip call” link beneath it. In the walkthrough the tester tapped Start, allowed the microphone and spoke. The orb glowed, but the coach never replied. After ending the call, the app showed “Analyzing Your Conversation” and then “Your plan is ready”, built on a conversation that never happened.

Why This Finding Matters

This screen is the activation moment. Of 201 members who reached it, 73 confirmed Skip (36%) and 114 started a call (client-provided). Members with a meaningful first call paid at 33% within 14 days; members who skipped paid at 17%, and members who started a call without a meaningful completion paid at 5% (client-provided). A silent call that ends in a “plan” puts a member in that last group at the moment the product is meant to prove itself, and it is logged as a completed onboarding rather than a failure. Nora has said she doesn’t want to force the call; the goal here is to make skipping less attractive than trying, and to make a failed try recoverable.
N-01 N-09 IXD-01 ONB-03 CRO-08
Recommendation
  • Show whether the coach is listening, thinking or speaking, and show a clear message if audio doesn’t connect within a few seconds.
  • Offer “Try again” and “Switch to text” when the coach doesn’t reply. The text coach already exists inside the app; surfacing it here turns a failed call into a second chance instead of a skip.
  • Skip “Analyzing Your Conversation” when nothing was said, and log these calls as failed rather than completed so the meaningful-call measure stays honest.
  • Replace “Skip call” with a secondary choice that keeps the member in a conversation: “I’d rather type” first, then a smaller “Skip for now”. Keep the “are you sure?” prompt and record how many people go back from it, which isn’t tracked today (client-provided).
  • Tell the member what the call is for in one line: two or three questions, about four minutes, and the plan on the other side is built from it.
Effort: Med Impact: High

Plan ready and paywall

4
Conversion Rate Optimization Major

“Your plan is ready” shows the same two lines to everyone, then the rating prompt, reminders and the paywall follow before the plan is used

After the call, “Your plan is ready” lists “Practice with Nervana AI” and “Go deeper when you want” with the three human coaches. Nothing on it reflects what the member just said: no symptom, no pattern, no first-session focus. The iOS rating prompt fires over this screen, the reminders request follows, and then the membership paywall. In this walkthrough the paywall itself was not captured because the tester had already paid on the website; on the website, choosing the monthly plan cut the trial from 7 days to 3 without saying so before the plan was picked.

Why This Finding Matters

This is where a member decides whether the call was worth a trial. Of 241 members, 151 reached the paywall and 73 started a trial (client-provided), so about half of the people who see the offer take it. The screen just before it is the only place the app can show that it listened, and today it doesn’t. Trial length matters at the offer too: 59.7% of observed trial cancellations happened within 24 hours, and monthly trials (100 of 147) last 3 days (client-provided). A plan that reads as personal, followed by an offer whose terms are clear, is the cheapest lift on this page.
CRO-10 ONB-07 COPY-02 IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Rebuild “Your plan is ready” from the call: the member’s primary symptom, one thing the coach heard, and what the first session will work on. Three lines, in the member’s own words where possible.
  • Move the App Store rating prompt to after the first full coaching session and debrief, and show it only to members who rated that session well.
  • On the paywall and on the website plan step, show the trial length on each plan before it is selected (“7 days free”, “3 days free”) with one line on why they differ, and say what happens on the last day.
  • Rewrite “practice safety” as “help your body feel safe”.
  • Record the in-app paywall in the next walkthrough so the offer itself can be rated.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Today

5
Onboarding & Activation Major

The first coaching session sits behind a five-slide tour, a seven-part lesson, a cost survey and a check-in

Today opens under a five-slide modal that only moves forward. Beneath it, “We’ll start with some preparation before our call”: the active step is a seven-part lesson, with Care Burden Baseline (urgent care visits, appointments, missed days and out-of-pocket spending over 90 days) and Weekly Check In locked below it, with no reason given. Coaching appears only after all three are done. In the walkthrough that took 32 minutes, and by the check-in the tester said it felt like preparing for something that hadn’t arrived yet.

Why This Finding Matters

The coach is what members are trialing, and a monthly trial lasts 3 days. Within 14 days, 67 of 241 members completed the Session 1 lesson, 60 the check-in and 56 the coaching session (client-provided), so each required step ahead of the coach is where some of them stop. Nora has already described the cost survey as data collection for insurance pathways rather than member value. Financial questions before the first session cost trust in a health flow, and the tour repeats what the Prepare, Session, Debrief track on Today already shows.
ONB-01 ONB-02 ONB-03 ONB-07 IA-10 N-03
Recommendation
  • Unlock Coaching the moment onboarding ends, and offer the lesson as optional preparation the member can do before or after the session.
  • Move Care Burden Baseline to after the first coaching session, or into week two, and explain the four icons or remove them.
  • Replace the tour with a one-line hint on each Today step the first time it appears. If a tour stays, cut it to three slides and allow swiping back.
  • On each locked card, say what unlocks it and how long it takes.
  • Show the reminders screen after the first session, when “One small practice is ready” has something behind it; 88 of 169 members skipped it where it sits today (client-provided).
Effort: High Impact: High

First coaching session

6
Nielsen’s 10 Heuristics Major

The coach has no way out when a member doesn’t see their symptoms as mind-body, and the session has no mute, pause or timer

In the first coaching session, the tester described their pain in physical, structural terms more than once. The coach returned to a nervous-system explanation each time, and nothing in the session lets the member say the framing doesn’t fit. During the 10 to 15 minute call the screen shows the orb, a background brush and End Conversation: no mute, no pause, no time elapsed.

Why This Finding Matters

This is the session that has to earn the second day, and second-day return is Nora’s repeat-value candidate (43 of 241 completed actions on two dates in week one, client-provided). One member wrote after a voice session that it “makes me feel heard and understood”; another wrote at session ten that the app “makes me feel like all I need to do is think different and I will be cured” and asked for physical causes to be acknowledged (client-provided). The same coach produces both, and the difference is whether it can hold a member’s own explanation without dropping the method.
N-02 N-03 N-01
Recommendation
  • Give the coach a path for members who attribute symptoms to a diagnosed physical cause: acknowledge it, point to their clinician, and offer a practice that doesn’t depend on agreeing.
  • Add a “This doesn’t fit me” option during or after the session, and review transcripts where a member repeats the same point three or more times.
  • Add mute and pause controls, and show time elapsed against the 10 to 15 minute session.
Effort: Med Impact: High

Methodology

Automated evaluation of Nervana’s public surface plus a structured senior-designer walkthrough of the signup to first coaching session flow, recorded on September 8, 2026 with one test account on one iPhone, rated across ten UX dimensions against a criterion-referenced rubric (Activation Score profile; Accessibility omitted for this native app, SEO and performance out of scope behind auth). Client-provided analytics were used to prioritize, never to score. The walkthrough predates the September 16 production update (1.0.56), which changed the opening headline and the post-call human-coaching copy; those screens should be re-recorded before the Teardown. The in-app paywall was not reached in this walkthrough because the tester had already subscribed on the website. A free Preview Score was produced on September 14 from the public surface only; it rated what could be seen from outside and is not comparable to this score.

Evaluation Lens

Nervana has to show that its AI coach is worth a trial inside a single sitting, to adults who have often tried a lot already, and most of them meet the product in the app rather than on the website. Each step was judged by whether it moves a new member toward a meaningful first conversation with the coach, whether the offer that follows is clear, and whether the first paid day makes them want a second.
