Why This Matters

Nervana's AI coach is what new members are deciding whether to keep paying for, and they decide inside a free trial. In this recording, a new member moves through an eight-screen story, a fit screener, sign-up, intake, a short free AI session, a rating prompt, a five-slide tour, a seven-part lesson, a cost survey and a check-in before the Coaching card unlocks. Findings

Top 5 Findings

The 5 highest-impact friction points, in the order a user hits them, grouped by page across the flow. Selected from a 15-finding evaluation across 9 UX dimensions. Select "See more details" on any finding for the evidence and fix.

Intro story

1
Onboarding & Activation Major

Eight story screens run before the app asks its first question

After Begin, the app plays an eight-screen story: "You are not alone", "The search for relief can take a toll", how Nervana works, the clinical advisory board, and a founder video with Education, Career and Personal sections. The progress bar tracks those eight screens, then disappears when the goals question starts, so the intake that follows gives no sense of how many steps remain.

Why This Finding Matters

The founder video and the advisory board are real trust assets. Placed up front, they push the first thing a member does for themselves, answering a question, to screen ten. Without a progress bar, the longer intake that follows feels open-ended.
ONB-02ONB-03IA-03
Recommendation
  • Cut the story before the first question to three screens: what Nervana is, how a session works, and who built it.
  • Move the founder video and the advisory board to a card on Today or the Coaches tab, so members can open them when they want.
  • Run one progress indicator from the first story screen through the free AI session.
Effort: Med Impact: High

Fit screener

2
Content & Copywriting Major

The fit screener is dense, and checking nothing leads to a stop screen before sign-up

"Which patterns feel familiar?" lists nine statements, several of them long and personal, such as "If you learned that a child you care about was growing up exactly as you did, you would feel sad or angry." Continue stays active with nothing checked. Tapping it that way opened "Let's check one thing first", which recommends talking with a healthcare provider to rule out structural causes before continuing.

Why This Finding Matters

This screen comes before account creation, so anyone who stops here leaves with no account and no way for Nervana to follow up. A member who is unsure, or who doesn't recognize themselves in the wording, gets the same response as someone the screen is meant to redirect. The provider recommendation belongs in the flow; it works better as guidance a member can act on than as the only answer to an empty screen.
COPY-06COPY-07N-05FORM-01
Recommendation
  • Add a "None of these" and an "I'm not sure" option, and route "I'm not sure" forward with a short explanation.
  • Shorten each statement to one plain line, and keep the longer detail behind "Why we ask".
  • Keep the provider recommendation, and let members create an account from that screen so they can come back after seeing their provider.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Sign up and intake

3
Forms & Input Major

Symptoms and history are collected before the privacy promise and the Terms

The sign-up screen reads "By creating an account, you'll also receive Finding Nervana, Nora's newsletter. Unsubscribe anytime." with no way to decline. The next screens ask for date of birth, gender, symptoms, the primary symptom and how long it has lasted. "Your story stays yours", which explains secure storage and de-identification, and the required Terms checkbox come after all of that.

Why This Finding Matters

Nervana already has the reassurance a health buyer looks for. Right now it arrives after the member has shared their most sensitive details, and just after an automatic newsletter sign-up. Putting the privacy promise first lets it build trust before the questions, when it counts.
ONB-07FORM-01VD-08
Recommendation
  • Move "Your story stays yours" and the Terms checkbox to directly after sign-up, before date of birth.
  • Add an unchecked newsletter opt-in to the sign-up screen, and apply it to Google and Apple sign-in too.
  • Add one line under the symptom question saying how the answer shapes the plan.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Plan ready and tour

4
Interaction Design Major

Three interruptions stack up between "Your plan is ready" and the first action

Right after the free AI session and "Analyzing Your Conversation", the App Store rating prompt appears over "Your plan is ready". A reminders screen and the iOS notification dialog follow. Today then opens under a five-slide modal tour that explains sessions, preparation and human coaching.

Why This Finding Matters

A member who has just talked with the coach for the first time is at a high point of motivation. A rating request before they have used the app spends a prompt that iOS allows only a few times a year. The modal tour explains the Prepare, Session and Debrief track that Today already shows on screen.
ONB-01ONB-07IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Ask for an App Store rating after a member finishes their first full coaching session and debrief.
  • Replace the modal tour with a one-line hint on each Today step the first time it appears.
  • Show the reminders screen after the first lesson, when "One small practice is ready" has something behind it.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

Today and first coaching session

5
Onboarding & Activation Major

The first coaching session sits behind a lesson, a cost survey and a check-in

Today opens with "We'll start with some preparation before our call." The active step is a seven-part lesson, and Care Burden Baseline and Weekly Check In sit below it with lock icons. The Coaching card appeared only after all three were done, including a baseline that asks about ER visits and out-of-pocket spending over the past 90 days.

Why This Finding Matters

The coach is what members are trialing. The free onboarding session gives a first taste, but the first full coaching conversation takes three more tasks, and the locked cards don't say why they are locked or how long each one takes. Each of those steps is a place a member can stop partway through a trial.
ONB-02ONB-03ONB-07IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Unlock Coaching right after onboarding, and offer the lesson as optional preparation.
  • Move Care Burden Baseline to after the first coaching session.
  • Add a time estimate to every Today card, and say what completing it unlocks.
Effort: High Impact: High
What The Research Says

Patterns that bear on this flow

Documented research and case studies matched to the weakest areas of this flow: what moved the numbers for comparable products.

Cutting onboarding to the shortest path to first value

D7 retention 9% to 23%

A HIPAA-compliant telehealth app rebuilt a five-step onboarding into two screens and deferred sign-up until after members saw value. Seven-day retention rose from 9% to 23% and primary conversion rose 38%. Nervana's story, intake, tour and preparation steps all come before the first full coaching session. These are self-reported agency figures, so treat the direction as the signal rather than the exact numbers.

Source: Fora Soft, telehealth video app UX rebuild

Establishing trust before asking for sensitive information

42% abandonment

An AI health companion saw 42% abandonment when it asked for sensitive data too early, and moved privacy badges and progress transparency to the front of onboarding. In this recording, symptoms, duration and date of birth come before the screen that explains how Nervana stores and de-identifies a member's story, and the progress bar has already disappeared.

Source: August AI, trust-driven healthcare onboarding
