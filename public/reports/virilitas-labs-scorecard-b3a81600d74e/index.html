Activation Scorecard

Virilitas Labs

virilitaslabs.com  ·  September 3, 2026

Why This Matters

Virilitas Labs has assets most health startups would trade for: peer-reviewed science, credentialed founders, and a live app in both stores. Right now, the path from first visit to an activated user hides those assets at every step where an anxious fertility patient decides whether to trust you, and the app's biggest moment of effort, a 23-question health assessment, ends without giving anything back. Every visitor who bounces there is an install, a research participant, and a future consultation that never happens.

Friction Areas

Priority Friction Areas

The six highest-priority friction points on the first-run activation flow, covered in depth — in the order a user hits them, grouped by page. Each is evidence behind a dimension rating above. Select "See more details" on any card for the evidence and fix. Every other issue we logged is listed under Additional Findings below.

Website — Homepage

1
Conversion Entry Critical

The homepage never asks for the install, then argues against it

The hero contains a headline and a photo, and nothing else: no button, no store badge, no link. One scroll later, the page's most visually dominant section says "Join our waitlist… be the first to know when we launch," directly beside sections linking to the live App Store and Google Play listings.

Why This Finding Matters

For a product-led app company, the homepage is the top of the entire funnel. A visitor primed to install reads "when we launch" and reasonably concludes the app is not out yet, so the strongest section on the page actively suppresses your primary conversion. Fixing this costs a headline and a button, and it touches every visitor you will ever get.
CRO-01 CRO-02 CRO-05 N-04
Recommendation
  • Put the primary action in the hero: store badges or a single "Get the app" button, with a secondary "For clinics" link for the B2B audience.
  • Rescope the waitlist section explicitly to the clinic diagnostics ("Clinics: be first to know when our diagnostics launch") or convert it into a patient newsletter signup. Never show "when we launch" beside live store badges.
  • Restyle primary buttons in a darkened brand teal with white text so they no longer read as disabled controls.
Effort: Low Impact: High
2
Onboarding & Activation Critical

The install is requested sight unseen: no product preview anywhere

The only activation path on the site is a pair of store badges. Across all four pages there is not a single app screenshot, demo clip, sample insight, or "how it works" step. The entire product description is one sentence, and every image on the patient-facing pages is stock photography.

Why This Finding Matters

An app install is a high-cost ask on a sensitive health topic where trust thresholds are highest. Visitors cannot form a mental model of what they are installing, and the credibility you have earned in the lab (peer-reviewed publications, an innovation award, credentialed founders) is invisible at the exact moment the decision is made. Every competitor we benchmarked shows the product before asking for anything.
ONB-02 ONB-03 CRO-08 VD-06
Recommendation
  • Add an app showcase section high on the homepage: two to three real app screenshots (Education Hub, AI chat, Community), a 20 to 30 second screen-recording clip, and three outcome-framed bullets.
  • Pair the store badges with a low-commitment preview ("See how it works") so undecided visitors have a step before install.
  • Surface a trust strip near the app CTA: built by University of East Anglia reproductive scientists, research published in PNAS, Innovation & Impact Award 2023.
Effort: Medium Impact: High

Website — Mobile first visit

3
Mobile UX Critical

The mobile first impression is a consent wall over a headline-less page

On a phone, the cookie banner covers roughly three quarters of the screen on arrival, and opening the menu renders the navigation underneath it, unreachable. Separately, the hero headline ships invisible and waits for a script-driven animation, so a first mobile visit can show an empty white frame where the headline should be.

Why This Finding Matters

Most high-intent traffic for an app company lands on mobile: ads, social, and App Store cross-links. Right now that first impression is a legal interstitial followed by a page that looks unfinished, on a brand asking users to trust it with fertility data. Both halves of this are small, contained fixes.
MOB-08 N-03 N-01 CRO-01
Recommendation
  • Switch to a compact bottom-sheet cookie banner (about a quarter of the viewport at most, as on desktop) that never overlays the opened navigation.
  • Make hero content visible by default and let the script add the entrance animation, so no real-world state ever renders the headline invisible.
Effort: Low Impact: High

App — First open

4
Onboarding & Activation Major

The app opens with no onboarding: no welcome, no orientation, no first task

A first-ever open drops the user straight into the Education Hub article list. There is no welcome, no expectation setting, no tour of the four tabs, and no suggested first action. In our recorded session, an "Update Available" dialog interrupted within seconds of the very first launch.

Why This Finding Matters

New users decide in the first session whether this app is for them. Without orientation, the value proposition (education, community, AI guidance, expert consultations) has to be discovered by accident, and the features most likely to retain a user, like Community and the assistant, may never be found. Health onboarding redesigns show some of the largest documented activation lifts in the vertical.
ONB-01 ONB-04 ONB-06
Recommendation
  • Add a short, skippable first-run sequence: what Virilitas is for, the four tabs in one screen each, and one clear suggested first action per user goal.
  • Keep it re-findable: a "Getting started" entry in the Profile so dismissed help can be recalled.
  • Suppress the update prompt on the first-ever launch; ask on a later session instead.
Effort: Medium Impact: High

App — Account creation

5
Bugs & Defects Critical

Signup dead-ends into an email round trip, then asks the user to log in again

After completing the signup form, users are told to leave the app and verify their email before logging in. In our recorded session this detour took the user out of the app for over half a minute, and the verification email's "Open App & Login" button landed on a blank sign-in form, requiring username and password to be typed again by hand.

Why This Finding Matters

This is the single most drop-off-prone pattern in signup design: every exit from the app is a chance to never come back, and every re-typed credential multiplies it. Users who arrived with intent are being lost to a mechanical step that competitors have reduced to a single email field or removed entirely with Apple and Google sign-in.
FORM-01 ONB-07 N-09
Recommendation
  • Add Sign in with Apple and Google, which removes both the verification round trip and the password for most users.
  • If email verification must stay, let users into the app immediately and verify in the background, or make the email's button deep-link into an authenticated session.
  • Let users sign in with their email address, not a separately remembered username.
Effort: Medium Impact: High

App — AI assistant & health assessment

6
Onboarding & Activation Major

The flow's biggest ask pays off with an empty text box

The 23-question health assessment is the largest effort the app requests, and it is genuinely well built. But completing it returns the user to the same generic chat welcome they saw before: no summary, no personalized insight, no acknowledgment of the answers just given, and no suggested prompts. The aha moment the assessment sets up never arrives.

Why This Finding Matters

This is where activation is won or lost in your product. A user who has just disclosed sensitive health details is maximally invested and maximally expectant; giving nothing back teaches them the effort was not worth it. The fix converts work you have already done (the assessment and the AI) into the moment users will remember and return for.
ONB-03 ONB-06 IXD-04
Recommendation
  • On assessment completion, open the chat with a personalized summary: what the answers suggest, two or three areas worth exploring, framed within the existing medical disclaimer.
  • Add three to five tappable suggested prompts to the chat's empty state, personalized from the assessment where available and generic before it ("What does a semen analysis measure?", "What lifestyle changes improve sperm quality?").
  • Explain the message quota ("50 messages remaining") the first time it appears, so it reads as generous rather than as a meter already running down.
Effort: Medium Impact: High
