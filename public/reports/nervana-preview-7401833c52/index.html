Findings

Top 5 Findings

The 5 highest-impact friction points, in the order a user hits them — grouped by page across the flow. Selected from a 16-finding evaluation across 9 UX dimensions. Select "View Details" on any finding for the evidence and fix.

Welcome and intro story

1
Onboarding & Activation Major

An eight-step story runs before the app asks anything

After “Begin,” the app moves through eight narrative screens: you are not alone, the search for relief, what traditional care can miss, learned patterns, the good news, your path with Nervana, the clinical advisory board, and a founder video with three expandable sections. The first question comes after all of them. A progress bar at the foot of these screens counts the eight steps.
Why This Finding Matters

Members who reach the app have already paid for a trial on the web, and most of them have already read this story on the marketing site. On a three-day monthly trial, every screen before the first question is time spent on material the member has seen, not on the coach they are paying to try.

ONB-02 ONB-03 IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Cut the pre-question story to three screens: what Nervana is, how a session works, and who built it.
  • Move the founder video and the advisory board to a card on Today that members can open when they want it.
  • Skip the story entirely for members who arrive with an active trial from the website.
Effort: Med Impact: High

Sign up

2
Forms & Input Major

Creating an account also subscribes the member to the founder's newsletter

Under the password field, the sign-up screen reads “By creating an account, you'll also receive Finding Nervana, Nora's newsletter. Unsubscribe anytime.” There is no checkbox to decline, and the Google and Apple buttons carry the same terms.
Why This Finding Matters

A few screens later the app promises that a member's story stays theirs. Bundling marketing email into account creation works against that promise at the exact moment Nervana starts asking for symptoms and history, and it is the kind of detail that shapes whether a health buyer trusts the rest of the flow.

FORM-01 CRO-08 VD-08
Recommendation
  • Add an unchecked newsletter opt-in to the sign-up screen, and keep it unchecked for Google and Apple sign-in too.
  • Offer the newsletter later, from Profile or after a member's first week.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

Member experience

3
Content & Copywriting Major

The headline number has no source, and the refund needs ten sessions

The screen leads with “90% of people who use Nervana find it helpful by their 10th session,” with no sample size, time frame or source. Directly beneath it, the money-back promise asks members to complete 10 Nervana AI sessions before a refund. The monthly plan's trial is three days, and the app's own goal picker recommends one session per week.
Why This Finding Matters

Specific numbers build trust when members can check them and reach them. At the pace Nervana itself recommends, session ten lands several weeks after the trial has already converted to a paid subscription, so the strongest reason to stay sits on the far side of the billing decision.

COPY-08 CRO-07 CRO-10
Recommendation
  • Add the basis for the 90% figure in a tap-to-expand note: who was asked, how many, and when.
  • Pair it with something a member can reach inside the trial, such as what members typically notice after their first session.
  • State when the money-back window closes, so members can see how it relates to billing.
Effort: Low Impact: High

Your plan is ready

4
Onboarding & Activation Major

The App Store rating prompt fires before the member has used the app

The system prompt “Enjoying Nervana? Tap a star to rate it on the App Store” appears over the plan screen at the end of setup, before the first lesson and before the first coaching conversation.
Why This Finding Matters

Members asked to rate an app they have not used will dismiss it or rate the setup they just finished. iOS caps how often this prompt can appear, so spending it here gives up the chance to ask after a good coaching session, which is when a rating would actually help App Store conversion.

ONB-07 IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Trigger the rating request after a member finishes their first coaching session and debrief.
  • Show it only to members who gave positive feedback on that session.
Effort: Low Impact: Med

Today dashboard

5
Onboarding & Activation Critical

The coaching conversation sits behind a lesson, a survey and a check-in

On first open, Today shows a Prepare, Session, Debrief track with a seven-part lesson as the active step. Care Burden Baseline and Weekly Check In sit below it with lock icons. The Coaching card appears only once all three are complete.
Why This Finding Matters

The AI coach is what members are paying to try. Reaching it took an eight-step story, roughly fifteen setup screens, a five-slide tour, seven lesson slides, a four-part cost survey and a check-in. On a three-day trial, each gate is a place where a member can stall before meeting the one thing that would make them keep the subscription.

ONB-02 ONB-03 ONB-07 IXD-05
Recommendation
  • Unlock Coaching right after onboarding, and offer the lesson as optional preparation.
  • Move Care Burden Baseline to after session one, or into week two.
  • Put a time estimate on every Today card, for example “Lesson · 6 min.”
Effort: High Impact: High
What The Research Says

Patterns that bear on this flow

Documented research and case studies matched to the weakest areas of this flow — what moved the numbers for comparable products.

Collapsing a multi-step onboarding to the shortest path to first value

D7 retention 9% → 23%

A HIPAA-compliant telehealth app rebuilt its onboarding from five steps to two screens and deferred signup until after the member had seen value. Seven-day retention rose from 9% to 23% and primary conversion rose 38%. Nervana's flow runs in the opposite direction: an eight-step story, roughly fifteen setup screens and a five-slide tour all sit before the first coaching conversation. These are self-reported agency figures, so treat the direction as the signal rather than the exact numbers.

Source: Fora Soft, telehealth video app UX rebuild

Making setup fields optional and simplifying consent

Registration 14.2% → 38.9%

A health plan app made its name screen optional, removed consent checkboxes and replaced instructional text with illustrations. Registration completion went from 14.2% to 38.9%, and drop-off on the name screen fell by a factor of ten. Nervana's equivalents are the required free-text attribution field and the newsletter bundled into account creation, both of which ask members to accept something before they have received anything.

Source: Sequoia Health, onboarding redesign

Establishing trust before asking for sensitive information

42% abandonment

An AI health companion measured 42% abandonment when sensitive data was requested too early in onboarding, and recovered it by moving privacy badges and progress transparency to the front of the flow. In this recording, the pattern checklist, symptom picker and duration questions all run before the screen that explains how Nervana stores and de-identifies a member's story, and the progress bar disappears at the same point.

Source: August AI, trust-driven healthcare onboarding
